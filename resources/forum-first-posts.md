Drafts for main@groups.ai4ra.uidaho.edu. Nothing here has been posted. Each subject carries its hashtag. Post these before November 8 so that attendees find something under each tag.

Never paste the workshop key or the Narrate invite code into a post.

## 1. #UDM

**Subject:** #UDM Does the Unified Data Model describe your office?

The AI4RA Unified Data Model is a draft shared schema for research administration data, written by a small team at one institution. It can only become universal if people at other institutions read it and tell us where it gets their work wrong.

Browse it here: https://ui-insight.github.io/AI4RA-UDM/

Start with the Glossary tab for plain-language definitions and the Statuses tab for how proposals and awards move through their statuses. Does a definition differ from how your office uses the term? Does your office track something that is missing, or call something by a different name?

One sentence is plenty. Reply here, or start a new topic with #UDM in the subject line. Please share public material only.

## 2. #AILiteracy, Promptulus

**Subject:** #AILiteracy Promptulus: what did it teach you about your prompts?

Promptulus is an AI literacy trainer from AI4RA. Nine characters each coach one discipline of working with AI. Give one a prompt or a task, and it returns a rating, your strengths, and one principle to apply. Then you revise and try again.

Try it here: https://ai4ra.github.io/promptulus/

What task did you bring to it, and what changed after you revised your prompt? Reply here, or start a new topic with #AILiteracy in the subject line. Please share only what you could post on a public bulletin board.

## 3. #AILiteracy, Remembering Thamus

**Subject:** #AILiteracy Remembering Thamus: one question, five ways to answer it

Remembering Thamus asks one compliance question and answers it five ways, from the model's memory to reading the regulation live. All five sound equally confident. Only some of them can be checked.

Try it here: https://nate-layman.github.io/thamus/

Which answer would you have trusted? What did you have to already know to catch the wrong one? Reply here, or start a new topic with #AILiteracy in the subject line.

## 4. #DataScience, Data Crawler Carl

**Subject:** #DataScience Data Crawler Carl: what did your spreadsheet leave out?

Data Crawler Carl lets you upload a spreadsheet, ask a question in plain English, and see the query the model wrote to answer you.

Try it here: https://ui-insight.github.io/data-crawler-carl/

There is a small sample awards table here if you don't want to use your own: https://ui-insight.github.io/NCURARegionalWorkshop2026/files/awards_demo.csv

Ask for total federal funding by department, then ask what data quality issues it sees. What did you notice? Reply here, or start a new topic with #DataScience in the subject line. Please use public or made-up data only.

## 5. #DataScience, HERD Explorer

**Subject:** #DataScience HERD Explorer: what do you see in the national R&D data?

HERD Explorer is a free, open-source way to look through the NCSES HERD Survey data, FY 2012 to FY 2024, for every reporting institution. It runs in your browser. An AI coding assistant helped build it in about two weeks. Its national totals are checked against the tables NCSES publishes.

Explore it here: https://nate-layman.github.io/herd-explorer/

The Ask page lets you ask a question in plain English and shows the query behind each answer: https://nate-layman.github.io/herd-explorer/ask.html

What did you find when you compared your institution with its peers? Post anything that looks wrong too. Reply here, or start a new topic with #DataScience in the subject line.

**Subject:** #NARC Reasoning puzzles and thinking well with AI

NARC is my puzzle platform. Each puzzle pairs a sequence of colored grids with a short narrative clue that only helps when read figuratively. You need both to reach the answer.

Browse the puzzles and the Team Picks here: https://narc.insight.uidaho.edu/

Which puzzle stumped you, and how did you work it out? Did a model get there, and did its explanation hold up? Reply here, or start a new topic with #NARC in the subject line.
