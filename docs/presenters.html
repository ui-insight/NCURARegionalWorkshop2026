Workshop presenters

Meet the people leading the session.

Two presenters from the University of Idaho, one from data science and one from philosophy, bringing complementary views on how AI works and how to think clearly with it.

University of Idaho

Nathan Layman

Lead presenter, Session 1. Data Scientist, University of Idaho

nlayman@uidaho.edu

Brief bio

Dr. Nathan Layman is a Data Scientist at the University of Idaho, where he architects data lakehouse infrastructure and develops AI-assisted workflows for research administration. He leads data engineering efforts for the NSF-funded AI4RA project, building open-source tools that integrate institutional research data. His research applies machine learning to complex biological and public health challenges, with expertise in computer vision, natural language processing, and Bayesian modeling. He co-founded the AI4RA blog and has delivered multiple national conference presentations on scaling AI adoption in research administration. He holds a PhD in Biology from Washington State University.

University of Idaho

Bert Baumgaertner

Co-presenter, Session 2. Department of Philosophy, University of Idaho

bbaum@uidaho.edu

Brief bio

Dr. Bert Baumgaertner is Professor of Philosophy at the University of Idaho, where he co-directs the Modeling Core of the Institute for Modeling Collaboration and Innovation. His research spans social epistemology, philosophy of science, and computational modeling, with a focus on how beliefs spread through groups and how people and AI systems reason together. On the NSF-funded AI4RA project he builds AI ethics and AI literacy training for research administrators, including facilitated dialogue workshops. He created NARC (Narrative Augmented Reasoning Challenges), puzzles that test whether a story can help an AI model solve a problem. He received the university's Presidential Mid-Career Excellence Award in 2023 and holds a PhD in Philosophy from the University of California, Davis.
